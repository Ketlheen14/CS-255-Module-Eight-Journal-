# CS-255-Module-Eight-Journal-
1. Briefly summarize the DriverPass project. Who was the client? What type of system did they want you to design?
The client for this project was DriverPass, a company that wanted to improve driver education by better preparing students for their DMV driving tests. DriverPass noticed that many students were failing their driving tests due to a lack of preparation. To address this problem, the company wanted a web-based system, preferably hosted in the cloud, that would bring together online classes, practice tests, driving lessons, appointment scheduling, and customer management. The system also needed to support different users, including customers, instructors, the secretary, the owner, and the IT officer, with different levels of access based on their responsibilities. Another important requirement was receiving updates from the DMV regarding driving rules, policies, and sample questions to keep the training materials current.
2. What did you do particularly well?
I believe I did a great job gathering and organizing the client's requirements and developing a system design that addressed most of their needs. I was able to identify the different components of the system and understand how they would work together. I also created UML diagrams to represent the relationships between users, system functions, and processes. These diagrams helped me visualize how the system would operate and how different users would interact with it. I think one of my strengths throughout this project was being able to take the information provided by the client and turn it into an organized plan that could guide the development of the system.
3. If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?
If I could revise one part of my work, I would improve the UML use case diagram. Specifically, I would make a clearer connection between the DMV updates ,the practice tests, and training. Although DMV updates can affect different areas of the system, I believe they have a more direct impact on practice tests and the training because changes to driving rules, policies, and sample questions should be reflected in the materials students use to prepare for their exams. Making this connection more visible in the diagram would help demonstrate how the system keeps its educational content updated.
4. How did you interpret the user's needs and implement them into your system design? Why is it important to consider the user's needs when designing?
I interpreted the user's needs by reviewing the client's requirements and identifying the different components and relationships needed for the system to function. I started by drawing a class diagram by hand to understand how the different parts of the system were connected. This helped me visualize the relationships and better understand how the system would operate. After organizing my ideas, I transferred the diagram into Lucidchart.
I believe considering the user's needs is one of the most important parts of system design because the system is being developed for the people who will use it. The purpose of designing software is to make their tasks easier, solve problems, and improve their experience. To accomplish this, it is important to listen, observe, ask questions, and take notes before beginning the design process. Understanding what users need helps ensure that the final system will actually solve the problems it was created to address.
5. How do you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a system?
My approach to designing software begins with understanding the problem the client is trying to solve. For DriverPass, I considered the need for a web application that could work across different browsers and devices, a database to store customer information and appointments, and a login system that would provide different levels of access based on each user's role. I also considered how the system would track changes, manage instructor schedules, store lesson notes, and maintain accurate records.
In the future, I would continue gathering as much information as possible from clients before beginning the design process. One strategy I find particularly important is asking why. Understanding why a system needs to be developed, why certain features are necessary, and what problems users currently experience provides a clearer understanding of the project's purpose. I would also continue using hand-drawn sketches and UML diagrams to organize my ideas before creating the final design. I believe that having a clear understanding of the problem and defining what a successful outcome should look like are essential steps in developing software that meets the client's expectations.
